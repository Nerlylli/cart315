#### Reflection
Nothing that we create is going to be perfect the first time. The outcome we expect is not the outcome we will get, and that is why reflecting in real time is better than anticipation. 
This reminds me of when I was building my own Pong variation. 
	Before even starting, I had a bunch of ideas about the modifications that I would make to my version - Change the keys, change the aesthetic, add multiple balls, etc - in order to make it more difficult for the player. When I was finally done, I felt somewhat satisfied and thought that I was done. I presented it in front of the class on Sept. 24th, and got some good feedback and realized that I wasn't as done as I thought I was, and so I wrote myself a note of the next alterations I could add to make it more different 
	- Add a menu page; 
	- Add a winning screen after the player gets a certain amount of points;
	- Make balls spawn everytime the player scores two points,
	In the end, the only one that ended up in my last version was the third planned alteration, due to certain functions not being present for unknown reasons and my unfamiliarity with Unity. 
This also reminds me of the saying: "Never stop learning, because life never stops teaching"
	During the interview, Donald Schön describes something that "[...]was intended as a design tool was in fact a vehicle for learning..." Even the students who build it didn't really comprehend what they had made, but through other student's conversational interaction with the program, they could have their questions answered.
